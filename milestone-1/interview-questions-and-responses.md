## 1. Librarian

**Q1. Can you walk us through what a typical busy day looks like for you? What tasks take up the most of your time?**

- A large part of the day goes into issuing and returning books — every transaction is written by hand in the register, which takes time.
- Tracking down overdue items and managing library cards adds to the load; she often has to work on Sundays just to keep records up to date.

**Q2. When a member returns a book — especially one that was borrowed a few months ago — how do you find the original record and mark it as returned?**

- She uses the date on the member's library card to find the exact issue date, then locates the corresponding page in the register.
- If the book was issued months ago, she has to go to the archive room and retrieve the older register book for that time period, which is slow.

**Q3. How do you currently figure out which members have overdue books? What does that process look like?**

- She has to manually go through earlier pages of the register and look for entries that do not have a return mark.
- There is no quick way to see all overdue items at once — it requires going through everything page by page, sometimes across archived registers.

**Q4. When a book comes back damaged or a member says it is lost, how do you decide what fine to charge and how do you record it?**

- She refers back to the original issue entry to find the book's purchase price, then calculates the fine manually — half the price for damaged, one and a half times the price for lost.
- This depends on doing the math by hand each time, which is prone to error.

**Q5. If a member wants a book that is already issued to someone else, how do you handle that situation?**

- The member verbally asks the librarian to keep the book for them when it comes back.
- There is no formal process, so if multiple members want the same book, deciding who gets it first relies entirely on the librarian's memory.

**Q6. When members ask you to buy a new book that the library does not have, how do you keep track of those requests and decide which ones to act on?**

- Members make requests verbally, and the librarian jots them down or relies on memory — members have no way of knowing if their request was noted.
- Deciding which books to buy within the budget is done informally, based on how often a title gets mentioned.

**Q7. How do you keep track of all the newspapers and magazines the library subscribes to, and how do you calculate what to pay the sellers at the end of the month?**

- Subscription details — title, copies, price, and frequency — are memorized or kept in scattered notes, and the monthly payout has to be calculated manually for each title.
- Combining subscription costs with book purchase costs into a single month-end report is time-consuming and easy to get wrong.

**Q8. How do you manage your monthly spending on books and subscriptions — do you have a way to know how much of the budget is left before the month ends?**

- There is no running total — she adds up spending mentally or on paper as purchases happen through the month.
- The full month-end tally is done from scratch at the end of the month by going through all records.

## 2. Members (On-Campus and Off-Campus)

**Q1. When you enter the library looking for a specific book or topic, how do you go about finding what you need?**

- Physically searches the racks section by section, and if the book is not found, asks the librarian who checks the register or tries to recall from memory.
- It is common to leave without the book even when it is physically present, because there is no way to quickly confirm where it is.

**Q2. How do you currently keep track of exactly when your borrowed books are due so you can avoid late fines?**

- Relies on the date written on the library card at the time of issue — there are no reminders of any kind.
- For magazines, the due date is only in the librarian's register, not on the member's card, making it even harder to track.

**Q3. Can you describe your experience with getting a new library card — either when your old one gets full or when the academic year restarts?**

- Has to return all currently issued books before the card can be renewed, then submits the old card and receives a new blank one.
- The process requires a dedicated trip to the library and means losing access to books that have not yet reached their due date.

**Q4. If you really want to read a book but someone else has checked it out, what do you do to make sure you get it next?**

- Verbally asks the librarian to set the book aside when it comes back — there is no formal confirmation that this will happen.
- If multiple members want the same book, there is no clear or fair way to determine who gets it first.

**Q5. If you want the library to buy a specific book, or if you want to recommend a book you just read to other students, how do you currently share that?**

- Verbally tells the librarian about the book they want purchased, with no way of knowing whether it will be acted on.
- There is no way to share a book recommendation with other students directly.

**Q6. Can you walk us through what the process of renewing your membership looks like each year?**

- Has to visit the library in person to renew and must return all currently issued books before it can be processed.
- For off-campus members, renewal is also tied strictly to the academic year start in July — so someone who joined in January ends up paying the full fee again after just a few months.

**Q7. When you have an outstanding fine or need to pay your annual membership fee, how do you currently go about settling it?**

- Fines have to be paid in person at the library — there is no way to settle them remotely.
