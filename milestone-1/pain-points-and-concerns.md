# Current Workflows and Pain Points

This document outlines the current manual operations, workflows, and associated bottlenecks within the Rashtrotthana Vidya Kendra (RVK) campus library.

---

## 1. Librarian Workflows & Challenges

### 1.1 Membership Management

- **Workflow:**
  - Student/Member applies ➔ Librarian issues a physical library card (acts like a bank passbook).
  - The card tracks borrowing history. Capacity is limited to approximately 20 entries (front and back).
  - When full ➔ Librarian collects the old card ➔ Issues a new blank card.
  - *(Note: RVK campus students are default members; off-campus students pay an annual ₹100 fee, strictly tied to the academic year.)*
- **Pain Points:**
  - Constantly issuing and managing physical replacement cards is a major administrative overhead.

### 1.2 Issuing Books & Tracking Returns

- **Workflow:**
  - **Issuing:**
    - Member selects a book or magazine ➔ Librarian writes the transaction in a physical register: `Member Name`, `Item Name`, `Date of Issue`, and `Expected Return Date`.
    - Register pages are organized chronologically by issue date. For example, all transactions from June 28 fill pages 1–3; transactions from June 29 start on page 4 onwards.
    - A single register book typically lasts 2–3 months before it is full.
    - For magazines: the transaction is recorded only in the librarian's own register, not in the member's library card.
  - **Return Deadlines:**
    - The return deadline is set by the librarian's judgment at the time of issue:
      - **5 days** → Magazines only. A magazine can only be issued once it is at least half its publication cycle old (e.g., a monthly magazine after 15 days from arrival; a weekly magazine after 4 days from arrival).
      - **10 or 20 days** → Standard for most books. The librarian decides based on the book's demand and availability.
      - **30 days** → Reserved for specific cases where a member needs extended access, such as large biographies or novels for long-term reading or research.
      - High-demand books that the library needs to keep in circulation may be restricted to a 10-day deadline only, at the librarian's discretion.
  - **Returning:**
    - When a member returns an item, the librarian must manually locate the original issue entry in the register to mark it as returned.
    - If the item was issued weeks or months ago, the librarian must retrieve the correct archived register from the archive room and find the exact page by date.
    - Old registers cannot be discarded — they are the only permanent record of all past transactions. Over time, these accumulate into large piles of register books in the archive room.
- **Pain Points:**
  - **Overdue Tracking:** To find members who haven't returned items, the librarian must manually scan through previous pages across potentially multiple registers to identify entries without a "returned" mark.
  - **Return Lookup:** Finding the original issue record for a returned item is slow — especially for late returns from months ago, which require digging through archived registers to locate the exact page by date.
  - **Fines Calculation:** When a book is returned late, damaged, or declared lost, the librarian must manually calculate fines (0.5x book price for damaged, 1.5x book price for lost) by referencing the original book cost.

### 1.3 Book Catalog & Inventory Management

- **Workflow:**
  - The library catalog is maintained across multiple physical registers, segregated by category (e.g., Fiction, Non-Fiction, Self-Help, Academic, Biographies).
- **Pain Points:**
  - **No Availability Status:** A register confirms that a book is owned by the library, but gives no indication of whether it is currently on the shelf or issued out to a member.
  - **Complexity:** Managing multiple copies of the exact same book is highly complicated in a manual register.
  - **Accuracy:** Keeping records updated when books are permanently lost or damaged is time-consuming and prone to human error.

### 1.4 Managing Reviews & Reading Lists

- **Workflow (Reviews):**
  - Member writes a review on paper ➔ Submits to librarian ➔ Librarian pins it to the library display board.
  - Librarian routinely rotates older reviews out for new ones.
- **Workflow (Reading Lists):**
  - Librarian curates topic-based lists to encourage reading and writes them down in a register.
- **Pain Points:**
  - Old reviews are completely lost or very difficult to retrieve once removed from the board. There is no way to associate reviews with specific books for future readers.
  - Modifying curated reading lists in a physical register is messy and inflexible.

### 1.5 Budget & Book Purchase Management

- **Workflow:**
  - Library receives a monthly allowance of up to ₹10,000 from campus management.
  - New books are purchased based on verbal demands and requests from members.
  - At month-end, the librarian tallies all spending and submits the total to campus management, who reimburse the exact amount spent.
- **Pain Points:**
  - With limited funds, the librarian has to manually balance and prioritize the most frequently requested books against their prices, relying heavily on handwritten notes and memory.
  - There is no running total of how much has been spent so far in the current month, making it easy to accidentally overspend or underspend.

### 1.6 Subscription Management (Newspapers & Magazines)

- **Workflow:**
  - The library subscribes to 5 newspapers (2 copies each) and roughly 10-15 magazines (2 copies each, with varying frequencies — weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly).
  - Sellers deliver the subscribed titles. At month-end, the librarian manually calculates the total payout owed to each seller.
  - This subscription cost is combined with book purchase costs to compile the full month-end expense report for campus management.
- **Pain Points:**
  - There is no central record of all subscriptions. The librarian has to remember every title, copy count, price, and delivery frequency from memory or scattered notes.
  - Calculating monthly subscription costs is tedious — a weekly title incurs ~4 charges, a bi-weekly title ~2, and a monthly title 1. Doing this for all subscriptions by hand is error-prone.
  - Combining subscription costs with book purchase costs at month-end into a single accurate report is time-consuming and difficult to verify.

## 2. Member Workflows & Challenges

### 2.1 Searching for Books

- **Workflow:**
  - Member wants a specific book ➔ Physically searches rack-by-rack.
  - If not found ➔ Asks the librarian ➔ Librarian checks category registers or relies on memory.
- **Pain Points:**
  - Because there are thousands of books, the librarian cannot memorize the entire inventory. It frequently happens that a requested book is physically present but the member leaves empty-handed because they couldn't find it.

### 2.2 The Card Renewal Bottleneck

- **Workflow:**
  - Cards must be renewed either when the ~20 entry limit is reached, or at the start of every new academic year (July).
  - Off-campus students must pay the ₹100 annual fee again at the start of the academic session, even if they joined late in the previous session (e.g., January).
  - To get a new card ➔ Member must return **all** currently issued books ➔ Submit old card ➔ Receive new card.
- **Pain Points:**
  - If a member's card is full, they must return all currently issued books just to facilitate the renewal process — regardless of their due dates.
  - The rigid academic year fee structure means off-campus members who joined late in the year get very little value before having to pay the full amount again.

### 2.3 Reserving Unavailable Books

- **Workflow:**
  - Member discovers all copies of a desired book are currently issued to others.
  - Member verbally requests the librarian to hold the book for them upon its return.
- **Pain Points:**
  - There is no formal reservation queue. The librarian has to mentally remember who asked for a book first. When multiple members want the same book, it leads to confusion and an unfair, informal waitlist.

### 2.4 Requesting New Books

- **Workflow:**
  - Member wants a book not owned by the library ➔ Verbally tells the librarian ➔ Librarian jots it down in a request register.
- **Pain Points:**
  - Members have no visibility on whether their request is being considered, how many other people want the same book, or when it might be purchased.
