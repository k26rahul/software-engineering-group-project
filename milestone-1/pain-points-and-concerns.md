# Current Workflows and Pain Points

This document outlines the current manual operations, workflows, and associated bottlenecks within the Rashtrotthana Vidya Kendra (RVK) campus library. 

---

## 1. Librarian Workflows & Challenges

### 1.1 Membership Management
*   **Workflow:** 
    *   Student/Member applies ➔ Librarian issues a physical library card (acts like a bank passbook).
    *   The card tracks the borrowing history. Capacity is limited to approximately 20 entries (front and back).
    *   When full ➔ Librarian collects the old card ➔ Issues a new blank card.
    *   *(Note: RVK campus students are default members; off-campus students pay an annual ₹100 fee, strictly tied to the academic year).*
*   **Pain Points:** 
    *   Constantly issuing and managing physical replacement cards is a major administrative overhead.

### 1.2 Issuing Books & Tracking Returns
*   **Workflow:** 
    *   Member selects book ➔ Librarian manually writes the transaction in a physical register (`Member Name`, `Book Name`, `Date of Issue`).
    *   Members have a strict 10-day deadline to return books.
*   **Pain Points:** 
    *   **Overdue Tracking:** To find members who haven't returned books, the librarian must manually scan through previous pages of the register to identify entries without a "returned" mark.
    *   **Fines Calculation:** When a book is returned late, damaged, or declared lost, the librarian must manually calculate fines (0.5x book price for damaged, 1.5x book price for lost) by referencing the original book cost.

### 1.3 Book Catalog & Inventory Management
*   **Workflow:** 
    *   The library catalog is maintained across multiple physical registers, segregated by category (e.g., Fiction, Non-Fiction, Self-Help, Academic, Biographies).
*   **Pain Points:** 
    *   **Blind Spots:** Looking at a register tells the librarian a book is owned, but provides no immediate indication if it is currently on the shelf or issued out.
    *   **Complexity:** Managing multiple copies of the exact same book is highly complicated in a manual register.
    *   **Accuracy:** Keeping records updated when books are permanently lost or damaged takes significant time and is prone to human error.

### 1.4 Managing Reviews & Reading Lists
*   **Workflow (Reviews):** 
    *   Member writes a review on paper ➔ Submits to Librarian ➔ Librarian pins it to the library display board.
    *   Librarian routinely rotates older reviews out for new ones.
*   **Workflow (Reading Lists):** 
    *   Librarian curates topic-based lists to encourage reading and writes these lists down in a register.
*   **Pain Points:** 
    *   Old reviews are completely lost or very difficult to retrieve once removed from the board. There is no structured system to associate reviews with books for future readers.
    *   Modifying (adding/removing books) curated reading lists in a physical register is messy and inflexible.

### 1.5 Budget & Purchase Management
*   **Workflow:** 
    *   Library receives a fixed budget of ₹10,000 per month.
    *   Funds are distributed across daily newspapers, weekly/monthly magazines, and new books.
    *   New books are purchased based on the verbal demands and requests from members.
*   **Pain Points:** 
    *   With limited funds, the librarian has to manually balance and prioritize the most frequently requested books against their prices, relying heavily on handwritten notes and memory.

## 2. Member Workflows & Challenges

### 2.1 Searching for Books
*   **Workflow:** 
    *   Member wants a specific book ➔ Physically searches rack-by-rack.
    *   If not found ➔ Asks the Librarian ➔ Librarian checks category registers or relies on memory.
*   **Pain Points:** 
    *   Because there are thousands of books, the librarian cannot memorize the entire inventory. It frequently happens that a requested book is physically present in the library, but the member leaves empty-handed because they couldn't find it.

### 2.2 The Card Renewal Bottleneck
*   **Workflow:** 
    *   Cards must be renewed either when the ~20 entry limit is reached, OR at the start of every new academic year (July).
    *   Off-campus students must pay the ₹100 annual fee again at the start of the academic session, even if they joined late in the previous session (e.g., January).
    *   To get a new card ➔ Member must return **all** currently issued books attached to the old card ➔ Submits old card ➔ Receives new card.
*   **Pain Points:** 
    *   Extreme inconvenience. If a member's card is full, they must return all currently issued books just to facilitate the renewal process.
    *   The rigid academic year fee structure means off-campus students paying late in the year get very little value before having to pay the full amount again.

### 2.3 Reserving Unavailable Books
*   **Workflow:** 
    *   Member discovers all copies of a desired book are currently issued to others.
    *   Member verbally requests the librarian to hold the book for them upon its return.
*   **Pain Points:** 
    *   There is no formal reservation queue. The librarian has to mentally remember who asked for the book first. If multiple students want the same book, it leads to confusion and an unfair, manual waitlist.

### 2.4 Requesting New Books
*   **Workflow:** 
    *   Member wants a book not owned by the library ➔ Verbally tells the librarian ➔ Librarian jots it down in a request register.
*   **Pain Points:** 
    *   Members have no visibility on whether their request is being considered, how many other people want the same book, or when it might be purchased.
