# Requirements Gathering

This document outlines the methods used to gather requirements for the Library Management System. The process was led by Vidhatri, with the entire team contributing input and discussion throughout.

---

## 1. Informal Conversations

**Led by:** Vidhatri

- Spoke informally with the librarian and several library members to get a quick sense of their day-to-day experience.
- Questions were open-ended and conversational — no rigid structure.
- Also spoke with a few school teachers about how they guide students toward books and how the library fits into that process.
- Examples of questions asked:
  - *"How does your typical day in the library look?"*
  - *"What do you find most time-consuming or frustrating?"*
  - *"Do you ever recommend specific books to your students? How do they usually find them?"*

---

## 2. Naturalistic Observation

**Led by:** Vidhatri

- Visited the library and observed operations firsthand without interfering.
- Watched the librarian work through her regular tasks — issuing books, handling returns, managing member cards.
- Observed members searching for books across the racks.
- Observed students coming in to renew their library cards and return items.
- Asked spontaneous follow-up questions to students while present — based on what was happening in the moment.
- Also visited the campus office and had a quick conversation with the campus management accounting staff about how the library's monthly budget and reimbursement process is handled.

---

## 3. Formal Interviews

**Led by:** Vidhatri
**Interview questions:** Collectively prepared by the entire team

- Conducted semi-structured interviews with the librarian and four library members.
- Audio recordings are available for the librarian's interview and two of the member interviews.
- Interviews focused on workflows, pain points, and unmet needs.
- Examples of topics covered:
  - Step-by-step process for issuing and returning books
  - How fines and overdue books are currently tracked
  - What members wish the library did differently
- *Full interview transcripts and notes will be documented separately.*

---

## 4. Secondary Research

**Led by:** Entire team

- Read online articles and resources to understand how libraries work in general — not just the technical side, but the librarian's broader role.
  - One key insight was that a librarian's job goes beyond just issuing books — she is also responsible for encouraging a reading culture among members. This directly informed our decision to include engagement features like book reviews, ratings, and recommendations. *(Source: [An Introduction to School Librarianship – OpenLearn](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/education-development/an-introduction-school-librarianship?active-tab=description-tab))*
- Also looked at the feature sets of existing Library Management Systems to understand what is standard.
  - This helped us identify gaps and think of improvements specific to our context. For example, when the librarian adds a new book to the catalog, instead of typing all the details manually, she could scan the ISBN barcode and have the system auto-fill the title, author, and genre from an online database. *(Source: [Vidyalaya School Library Management System](https://www.vidyalayaschoolsoftware.com/features/school-library-management-system))*
